## Universal v5.18.0

- **Results:** dumped on both builds across the Universal dtypes, 915 results in
  mtl5-python and 381 arrays in universal_dtypes. The suites stayed green,
  and the differences were all fixes or intended changes:
  - in mtl5-python, a cascade's −0, and posit64 `norm2` now correctly
    rounded;
  - in universal_dtypes, three behaviour changes the suite had not caught
    (below).
- **GCC 11 can't compile Universal 5.13 and later** (constexpr
  `std::vector`). Both repositories' CI moved to `g++-12`, and CMake stops on
  GCC < 12.
- **universal_dtypes 3.0.0** is a major release, by the user's choice:
  - fp16 and fp8e5m2 now encode ±inf as IEEE does, so they match
    `numpy.float16` and `ml_dtypes` for every bit pattern;
  - q31 `sqrt` is correctly rounded (`sqrt(maxpos)` used to give −1);
  - a fixed-point x / 0 aborted the interpreter, because Universal now
    throws `fixpnt_divide_by_zero`. `FIXPNT_THROW_DIVIDE_BY_ZERO=0` restores
    saturation.
  - Its RELEASING.md gained a step: diff the results against the published
    wheel before bumping Universal.
- **mtl5-python refuses fp16 transport** when the two packages' builds
  straddle Universal 5.15. `build_info()["universal"]` reports the version.
  The ecosystem job failed against 2.5.1, as designed, and passed once 3.0.0
  was on PyPI.
- **The posit32 benchmark guard band** now depends on the Universal release,
  because posit arithmetic got 6.9x faster.

## The three issues

- **#151:** real matrices get `frobenius_norm` and `norm`. `ord=2` stays
  Frobenius, as MTL5's is; the docstrings point at `svdvals(A)[0]` for the
  spectral norm.
- **#152:** wider than reported.
  - What narrowed to float32: integer, bool and float16 arrays, but also
    float64 arrays in Fortran order, transposed, strided, or paired with a
    float32 operand. Strided float64 `norm` and `dot` were affected too.
  - The ndarray parameters are now `noconvert`. Python makes any layout
    contiguous, keeping the dtype, and refuses other dtypes with the cast to
    make, as the factorizations already did.
  - The first CI run failed the benchmark guard. The wrapper had added
    1.5 µs to the per-call floor. A fast path for calls with no NumPy
    argument brought that down to 0.1 µs.
- **#154:** a keyword-only `seed` (uint64) on the four random generators.
  It calls MTL5's portable seeded overloads.
